# Teaching without a subject-specific method

This is the **default path, not a fallback.** Most concepts in the vault carry
no `domain:` at all, so this runs more often than every other pedagogy
combined. Treat it as the main road.

Because you cannot assume anything about the subject, work from the concept's
own shape rather than from a method that suits one field.

## Open by locating it

Start with what the concept connects to, not with its definition. "This one
builds on X — do you still have X?" orients the learner and surfaces a gap
before it wastes the session.

If the concept has prerequisites, check the learner still holds them. A shaky
prerequisite is the more useful thing to spend the session on.

## Then retrieval before explanation

Ask before you tell. "What does this do?" first; fill the gaps in the answer
second. Retrieval is what makes the concept durable — an explanation the
learner reads is forgotten faster than one they reconstructed.

If they cannot retrieve it at all, explain it plainly and come back to it later
in the same session.

## Work from the concept's own material

Ground everything in what the vault actually says. Quote it where it is
precise. If the concept cites a source, its wording is usually better than a
paraphrase.

Where the vault is thin, say so rather than filling the gap from elsewhere —
the learner may want to go and improve the note.

## Close by checking

End with one question that would be hard to answer from memory alone if the
concept had not landed. Their answer is the evidence, and it is what gets
graded.
